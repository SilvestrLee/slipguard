## 2. What should remain unchanged

These are protected by `ADR-008`/`ADR-009`, `PD-007`/`PD-008`/`PD-009`, and Programme U-07's freeze. None of the findings below touch them:

- The evaluation model: every figure on screen is read from the last persisted `PlannerRegenerationEvent`, never recomputed live. This is architecture, not styling, and must not change.
- The lifecycle (`Draft → Evaluated ⇄ Evaluated → Complete → Exported`, `Abandoned` from any non-terminal state), and every transition rule.
- The original-slip lock (hard lock, read-only for the session's duration) and its collapsed-by-default disclosure panel.
- Compare Revisions being strictly customer-pulled (never auto-surfaced), and read-only (no "restore an earlier revision" — a known, already-accepted gap, not something this review reopens).
- The "no suggestions, customer types every replacement" rule for adding a selection — Capability A's defining boundary (`PD-007`). Nothing here should ever start to feel like the product is recommending a selection.
- Export creating an independent new `BettingSlip` (PD-009) with the original permanently preserved.

## 3. Findings

### 3.1 Cosmetic / UX polish (safe to modernise without touching logic)

1. **Button treatment is inconsistent within a single screen.** The Workspace mixes three distinct button styles on one page: the "Add selection" form button correctly uses `<x-primary-button>` (small, uppercase, `bg-gradient-button`); the main action row ("Confirm this selection", "Create a betting slip from this selection") is hand-rolled markup using a flat `bg-accent-strong` fill and sentence case, predating the founder's 2026-07-27 direct instruction to standardise on `bg-gradient-button` everywhere (`primary-button.blade.php`'s own doc comment records that instruction); and the Abandon confirmation modal correctly uses `<x-danger-button>`. The homepage's own CTAs (`public-nav.blade.php`, hero, Proof section) are larger, sentence-case, and gradient-filled — closer to the hand-rolled style here than to the small-uppercase one, but neither Workspace button style matches the homepage exactly. **Recommendation**: replace the hand-rolled action-row buttons with `<x-primary-button>`/`<x-secondary-button>` (or a size variant of them) so the whole screen — and ideally the whole authenticated portal — draws from one button vocabulary instead of three.
2. **No step/progress indication for a genuinely multi-step workflow.** UI UX Pro Max's own guidance (Feedback → Progress Indicators, Medium severity) flags exactly this pattern: a multi-step process (evaluate → adjust → compare → confirm → export) with no "step 2 of 4"-style orientation. The Workspace instead relies on the status line ("Status: Evaluated") and section headings alone. A light-touch, non-persuasive step indicator (matching `DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md`'s "calm, not exciting" personality — no progress bars implying urgency, per `MOTION_SYSTEM.md`'s explicit ban on scarcity-style bars) would help orient a customer returning to an in-progress session days later.
3. **Mobile truncation removes exactly the information a customer needs.** On a 390px viewport, the "your other selections" rows apply `truncate` to the "Event — Selection" label (e.g. "Man City vs Liverpoo…"), while "Keep this selection"/"Remove" sit in a fixed-width column beside it. On the narrowest devices this cuts off precisely the detail the customer is deciding whether to remove. **Recommendation**: stack label above actions on narrow viewports (consistent with `DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md`'s "single-column... every layout should work at 375px wide first" principle) instead of truncating.
4. **The Exported state is visually flat for what the workflow itself treats as a real completion moment.** Two plain text cards ("Your original slip" / "Your new slip") with no distinguishing visual weight — no icon, no colour, no risk-band summary of the finalised selection. `MOTION_SYSTEM.md` explicitly grants the risk-band-appearing moment "a slightly more deliberate reveal, because it's the product's core moment of truth"; completing an entire planning session and exporting is a comparable moment for this specific feature, currently under-weighted relative to how much workflow led up to it. **Recommendation**: a modest visual acknowledgment (e.g. a checkmark accent, the final risk band shown once more here) — never a celebratory or gamified treatment (no confetti, no "Congratulations!" — `DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md`'s Brand Tone forbids exclamation and hype).
5. **Minor copy oddity in the Primary Finding card**: "Without this selection, the slip's band would move from Low to Low" (or any case where removal doesn't change the band) reads as a copy bug even though it's mathematically correct — the sentence structure assumes a change. **Recommendation**: suppress or rephrase this specific line when `band_without_leg` equals the current band (e.g. "Removing this selection wouldn't change the overall band").
6. **The Analyze Slip index (the Planner's actual entry point) has not been touched by any of the recent premium-homepage work.** It is a dense, plain list — small badges, no card elevation, no icons distinguishing status, "Untitled slip" repeated dozens of times with no visual grouping. This is the first screen a customer sees before ever reaching the Planner, and it reads as the oldest part of the product next to the homepage's new Proof section, metric cards, and gradient CTAs. This is a pre-existing condition of the whole Betting Slip index, not something introduced by or unique to the Planner — flagged here because it is the Planner's literal front door.

### 3.2 Standardise with the new homepage design language (consistency work, not new capability)

7. **Card system**: the Workspace already reuses `shadow-elevation-1`/`shadow-elevation-2` and `bg-surface-card` correctly (no new card pattern needed), but the homepage's Proof section introduced an "always-visible explanation beneath the metric" convention (`metric-card.blade.php`'s new `explanation` prop, `PO-U15.6-001`) that the Planner's own Revision score card doesn't use — the risk band and score are shown with no explanatory line the way the homepage's metrics now have. Applying the same always-visible-explanation convention here (e.g. a one-line "why this matters" under the score) would bring the Planner in line with the homepage's own explainability standard it was already built to satisfy in spirit (Frame P-01's own intent).
8. **Fixed Atmospheric Layer parity confirmed correct, not a gap**: `MOTION_SYSTEM.md` explicitly names Planner Workspace among the authenticated screens that should carry the fixed atmospheric layer, and it does — verified present in both themes. Nothing to change here; recorded so this review doesn't leave it as an open question.
9. **The Reveal Animation convention (`data-reveal`, fade/rise-once) used sitewide on public pages and mentioned for "the risk report's headline/band appearing after analysis" is not applied to the Planner Workspace's own Revision score reveal.** Since `MOTION_SYSTEM.md` already treats "risk band appearing after analysis completes" as one of the two named reveal-animation use cases, and the Planner's Revision card is functionally the same moment (a structural score appearing after evaluation), this is a documented convention not yet extended here — not a new pattern to invent, just a gap in applying an existing one.

### 3.3 Genuine defects (found, not fixed, logged separately)

10. **Dark theme doesn't reach the shared header/hero band (mobile) or the page footer (desktop)** — reproduced independently on the Planner Workspace, the plain Dashboard, and the `/analyze` index, confirming this is a sitewide layout issue, not a Planner defect. Already logged as `INC-2026-003` (updated during this review with the additional footer sighting). Not fixed here — it is a shared-layout issue, not something a Planner-scoped commission should touch incidentally.

### 3.4 Would require a separately-commissioned enhancement (touches accepted functionality, explicitly not proposed for adoption here)

These are named only so the founder can see the boundary clearly — none are recommended, requested, or scoped by this review:

11. **A visible progress/step indicator** (finding 3.1.2) is cosmetic in principle but touches the Workspace's own template structure meaningfully enough (a new persistent UI region present across every session state) that it should be its own small commission with a mock reviewed first, not folded silently into a "polish pass."
12. **Restoring an earlier revision** (Compare Revisions' known, already-accepted gap, `PD-008`'s OI-1) is a backend/domain change (a new action, since `RemovePlannerSelection` currently hard-deletes rows), explicitly not a styling matter — this review does not reopen it, only notes it remains the same accepted gap it always was.
13. **Naming an exported slip automatically** (e.g. "{original name} (Planner)" instead of defaulting to unnamed) touches `ExportPlannerSession`/`SaveBettingSlip` behaviour, not presentation — a product decision (should the export inherit a name at all?) before an engineering one.
14. **Modernising the Analyze Slip index** (finding 3.1.6) is real but large enough — it is the shared entry point for Manual Entry, Paste Text, PDF Upload, Screenshot Upload, and the Planner all at once — that it deserves its own commission rather than being absorbed into a "Planner review."

## 4. Summary for the Product Office

- **Zero findings against the accepted deterministic Planner logic itself.** Everything the Planner calculates, persists, and enforces is untouched by this review and was not found wanting.
- **Six cosmetic/UX findings** (§3.1) are low-risk, template-only changes that would not touch `app/Actions/Planner/*` or `app/Domain/Planner/*` at all.
- **Three standardisation findings** (§3.2) would bring the Planner in line with conventions the homepage has already established this year, one of which (the atmospheric layer) is already correctly in place and needed no action.
- **One defect** (§3.3) is sitewide, not Planner-specific, and already logged.
- **Four items** (§3.4) are named explicitly as out of bounds for a styling-only pass and would need their own commissioning if the founder wants them pursued.

No code was changed as part of this review. If the founder wants any of §3.1/§3.2 implemented, that would be a new, explicitly-scoped Engineering commission — this document is the audit that would inform it, not the commission itself.
