No such acceptance entry exists for either.

This matters for this review's actual mission (§1) directly: the Background checklist's "✓ Planner session architecture" is accurate as a statement that the *design* exists — it is not yet accurate as a statement that the design is *constitutionally binding*. That gap is this review's first finding, not a redesign of anything.

## 1. Mission Confirmation

Per the handover: assess whether the Planner architecture remains internally consistent once `DR-01`, `DR-02`, and `DR-04` are resolved as currently proposed, and answer five specific questions. No redesign, no new capability, no new programme — confirmed in scope throughout.

## 2. Method

For `DR-01` and `DR-02`, "as currently proposed" is read as their recommended options in `docs/02-architecture/U-07.2-PLANNER-PRODUCT-DECISION-REGISTER.md` (DR-01: Option 2, neutral factual trend display; DR-02: Option 2, a generous soft cap). **`DR-04` cannot be read this way** — its own recommendation is *"defer this decision"*, not a specific retention policy — so §4 treats that distinction as a finding in its own right rather than silently picking a policy on Product Office's behalf.

## 3. Answers to the Five Explicit Questions

### Question 1 — Does the Planner architecture remain internally consistent once DR-01/02/04 are resolved as proposed?

**Not fully — two gaps exist that are independent of how DR-01/02/04 resolve, found during this review, not previously reconciled:**

**Gap A — Export target is ambiguous, and the ambiguity is new, not inherited.** `U-07.1`/`ADR-009` (written before `PD-008` existed) specify export as *overwriting the same slip in place*: `Ready → Draft → SaveBettingSlip → Draft → Ready` on the original `BettingSlip`. `PD-008` (decided after `U-07.1`) states the original slip "becomes the baseline reference against which all subsequent planner iterations are compared" and that "the Planner never rewrites history." Overwriting the original slip's legs at export *is* rewriting the one artefact `PD-008` calls a permanent baseline reference — the two decisions were never reconciled against each other, because `PD-008` postdates the architecture it now sits on top of. This is answerable only by Product Office (does export overwrite the source slip, or create a new one and leave the original untouched?), not by Architecture Office inventing a reading of `PD-008`'s own text.

**Gap B — The source-slip hard lock (`PD-008`/`DR-05`, Option 3) has no specified mechanism.** `U-07.2`'s own DR-05 entry already named this: "Options 2–3 both require E-03's builder to become aware of Planner session state — a small new coupling `U-07.1`'s design does not currently include (it keeps the two contexts one-directional)." `PD-008` selected Option 3, making this coupling mandatory, not conditional — but neither `ADR-009` nor any other document specifies *how* the builder checks for an open session, what happens when a locked edit is attempted, or how the lock is lifted. This is a real, concrete architectural gap, not a redesign of anything already decided.

**DR-01/DR-02, as proposed, introduce no inconsistency of their own:** DR-01's trend display reuses `PlannerRegenerationEvent`'s already-designed history exactly as specified; DR-02's soft cap needs only a count of existing events (already available via `sequence_number`), not a new mechanism.

### Question 2 — Are there remaining architectural ambiguities that would force Engineering to invent product decisions?

**Yes — precisely Gaps A and B above.** Stated explicitly, per the question's own instruction: without a decision on the export target, Engineering would have to guess whether "finish planning" destroys the customer's original entered slip; without a lock mechanism specification, Engineering would have to invent what a customer sees when they try to edit a slip mid-plan. Both are product-facing behavioural questions, not implementation details — Architecture Office cannot resolve either unilaterally without repeating the exact mistake this programme has twice already declined to make (`PO-E06D-001`, `PO-U13.1-001`'s predecessor pattern).

### Question 3 — Does any Planner component still contain hidden behavioural assumptions?

Identified, each traced to a specific document rather than asserted generally:

1. **Export overwrites the source slip** (`ADR-009`) — an assumption, not a decision, now in tension with `PD-008` (Gap A).
2. **The customer will simply be prevented from editing a locked slip with no further mechanism** (implicit in `PD-008`'s Option 3 selection) — no specification of *how* (Gap B).
3. **`DR-04` left "deferred" means "retain indefinitely" by default** — this is a real, if minor, hidden assumption: if Product Office does not make an explicit retention decision before implementation, Engineering will build against whatever the *absence* of a decision implies, which today is silent, unbounded retention — itself a policy, just an unstated one.
4. **`DR-02`'s cycle count source is unstated** — presumably `PlannerSelection`/`PlannerRegenerationEvent`'s own `sequence_number`, per `U-07.1` §6, but no document says this explicitly; a one-line confirmation would close this cleanly (minor, not blocking, unlike 1–3 above).

### Question 4 — Can Engineering implement the complete Planner without further Product Office clarification?

**No.** Three concrete blockers, none of them speculative:

1. **`ADR-008` and `ADR-009` are not Accepted.** Per this office's own constitution and the `ADR-007` precedent both ADRs explicitly invoke, Engineering has no constitutional authority to build against a Proposed ADR — this is a governance prerequisite, independent of DR-01/02/04 entirely, and the single most important finding of this review.
2. **Gap A** (export target) must be resolved — it changes what data model the export action actually needs (update-in-place vs. create-new-and-link).
3. **Gap B** (lock mechanism) must be specified at least at the behavioural level (what the customer sees, what triggers the lock's release) before Engineering can build the E-03/Planner coupling `PD-008` requires.

### Question 5 — After DR-01/02/04 close, will the Planner architecture be constitutionally complete?

**No — not from DR-01/02/04 closing alone.** Closing those three resolves the product-policy layer this review was asked to check, and does so cleanly (§3, Question 1's last paragraph). It does not, by itself, resolve Gaps A/B, and it does not retroactively accept `ADR-008`/`ADR-009`. All three of those are independent prerequisites this review found, not consequences of DR-01/02/04.

## 4. Outcome

**Outcome B, per the handover's own framework — a specific, objectively-justified set of dependencies, not a speculative one:**

1. Formal Product Office acceptance of `ADR-008` and `ADR-009` (or specific revision requests, if either is found wanting) — a pure governance step, no redesign implied.
2. A decision on Gap A: does Planner export overwrite the source `BettingSlip`, or produce a new one, leaving the original as `PD-008`'s "baseline reference" literally untouched forever.
3. A decision on Gap B, at minimum at the behavioural level: what a customer sees when attempting to edit a slip locked by an open Planner session, and what releases the lock.
4. `DR-01`, `DR-02`, `DR-04` closed (this review found no new obstacle to closing them as currently proposed, beyond the meta-point in §3.3 about `DR-04`'s deferred status).

None of these four require new architecture, new capability, or a new programme — three are decisions on top of the already-designed architecture, and one is simply accepting the design that already exists.

## 5. Constraints Compliance

No redesign proposed anywhere above — Gaps A and B are reported as open questions with the specific decision each needs, not resolved by this document. No optimisation, no new capability, no new programme, no invented product decision (Gaps A/B are *named*, not answered). Review only, as instructed.
