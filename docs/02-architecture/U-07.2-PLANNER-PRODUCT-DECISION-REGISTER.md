## 3. The Decision Register

### DR-01 — Planner Messaging Philosophy: Completion and Abandonment Guidance

**Background:** Consolidates `U-06.1` R3 (Accumulator Tax tension), `U-06.1` R4 (regulatory exposure), `U-07.1` OD-1 (proactive "no further improvement" messaging), OD-2 (proactive "consider abandoning" messaging), and PR-1/PR-5 (the same two risks restated). The Planner's stated purpose (SD-001) is deterministic, explainable help constructing an accumulator. The already-approved Accumulator Tax concept states the opposite emphasis — more legs compound structural risk. Architecture's orchestration design (`U-07.1` §6) makes a full score/band history across regeneration cycles available, but does not decide whether the Planner ever surfaces that trend as guidance rather than raw data.

**Current State:** No such messaging exists anywhere in the product today. SD-001 permits deterministic, explainable planning recommendations, but reaffirms: no outcome prediction, no safe-bet claims, no black-box suitability determination — "AI may only explain, in plain language, reasoning that is itself deterministic." `docs/09-compliance/PRODUCT_GUARDRAILS.md`'s Responsible Design section already prohibits "urgency designed to increase betting frequency" and "language that treats a risk score as permission to bet." That document's Transparency section already flags final legal/regulatory wording as requiring review before public launch — a standing requirement, independent of which option below is chosen.

**Available Options:**
1. **No proactive messaging.** The Planner displays the current score/band/ranking on request only; the customer alone decides when to stop or export.
2. **Neutral factual trend display.** Shows the score/band history across cycles (e.g., "Cycle 1: 64/High → Cycle 3: 41/Moderate") without any evaluative "should" language.
3. **Directive completion guidance only.** Adds something like "no further improvement found in recent cycles" — no abandonment-side messaging.
4. **Full directive guidance**, both completion- and abandonment-style (e.g., also surfacing that an accumulator has remained Very High risk across multiple cycles).

**Advantages / Trade-offs:**

| Option | Advantages | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| 1. None | Zero regulatory/messaging risk; simplest to build; cannot be read as a nudge to keep betting or as a suitability determination | Least helpful; customer must self-interpret raw numbers every cycle; weakest realization of "assists... through explainable planning" |
| 2. Trend only | More helpful without an evaluative claim; arguably the safest way to add value | Some customers may still read a shown trend as implicit guidance; exact display wording still needs Compliance review |
| 3. Completion only | Directly answers "am I done?"; reuses only already-deterministic facts, no new judgment | Suitability-adjacent statement; sharpens the Accumulator Tax tension; needs Compliance sign-off on trigger condition and wording |
| 4. Full guidance | Most complete customer guidance; gives the product an explicit voice for genuinely high-risk constructions | Highest regulatory exposure of the four; closest to advisory/tipster-adjacent territory SD-001 itself flags; hardest to keep from reading as a prediction even when structurally framed |

**Product Implications:** *Customer Experience* — determines whether the Planner ever "speaks" beyond showing numbers, shaping the product's perceived personality. *Implementation* — Architecture already persists everything any of the four options need (`U-07.1` §6); no re-architecture required regardless of choice. *Explainability* — Options 3–4 need new, Compliance-reviewed copy patterns; Options 1–2 need none beyond `docs/05-ux/EXPLAINABILITY_SYSTEM.md`'s existing rules. *Future Roadmap* — sets precedent for whether SlipGuard ever offers proactive guidance elsewhere (History, Journal), not only the Planner.

**Recommendation (optional):** Option 2 balances real customer value against the regulatory exposure and Accumulator Tax tension already flagged twice in this repository's history, without foreclosing Options 3–4 once the standing pre-launch Compliance/legal review has actually happened. Recommend starting that review now, in parallel with this decision, since its findings may narrow which option remains viable.

### DR-02 — Regeneration Cycle Limit

**Background:** `U-07.1` OD-3. The orchestration pipeline allows unlimited lock/unlock/remove/replace/re-evaluate cycles per session; Architecture recommended a simple structural counter exist as a safeguard, leaving its existence and value to Product Office.

**Current State:** No cap exists in any design or code today. `config('slipguard.max_legs')` (currently 20) is the closest existing precedent — an already-established configurable numeric ceiling, not a new mechanism.

**Available Options:**
1. **No cap.**
2. **Generous soft cap** — a warning past a large number of cycles, never blocking.
3. **Hard cap** — the session is forced to `Complete` or `Abandoned` past a fixed number of cycles.

**Advantages / Trade-offs:**

| Option | Advantages | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| 1. None | No artificial limit on genuine deliberation | No protection against a runaway loop or unbounded history-row growth |
| 2. Soft cap | Protects against runaway growth without ever blocking a deliberate customer | The number has no natural anchor; a non-blocking warning may not fully address the operational concern |
| 3. Hard cap | Simple, predictable ceiling for storage/performance planning | Could interrupt a genuinely careful customer; the cutoff is arbitrary |

**Product Implications:** *Customer Experience* — only Option 3 is a visible hard stop. *Implementation* — trivial regardless of choice (a counter and comparison); does not affect the lifecycle enum. *Explainability* — if a cap exists, hitting it must be explained in plain language, never a silent block. *Future Roadmap* — minimal; easily revised later.

**Recommendation (optional):** Option 2, reusing the `max_legs`-style configurable-constant pattern — the exact number remains a genuine product choice.

### DR-03 — Planner Entry Point: Existing Slip Only, or From-Scratch Entry — **RESOLVED (`PD-007`, 2026-07-26)**

**Resolution:** `PD-007` ("SlipGuard's responsibility begins only after a customer has supplied a betting slip for analysis") resolves this to **Option 1 — existing-slip-only**, recorded in `docs/00-governance/DECISION_LOG.md`.

**A reading worth stating plainly, not silently assumed:** `PD-007`'s explicit text answers a broader, already-settled question (the Planner never generates, discovers, or recommends selections — Capability A's customer-sourcing boundary, now stated in stronger, more permanent language than `PO-U06.1-AC-001`'s "deferred to a future programme"). That boundary is true under **all three** options below equally — even Option 2's from-scratch entry would still be 100% customer-typed, never system-generated. What resolves DR-03's actual question (existing-slip-first vs. a Planner-native entry screen) is the more specific phrase "a customer has *supplied a betting slip*" — `docs/00-governance/PRODUCT_GLOSSARY.md` defines "Betting Slip" as a specific, already-assembled artifact, and "supplied... for analysis" mirrors the existing Risk Engine's own "Ready slip" precondition language exactly. That phrasing is satisfied by Option 1 and not naturally by Option 2 (where the slip would still be *being* assembled while the Planner is already active, not yet "supplied"). Recorded on this basis; if `PD-007` intended to leave the entry-mechanism question open rather than settle it, that is a one-line, low-cost correction to this record — nothing has been built against it.

**Background (original, retained for context):** `U-07.1` OD-4. Architecture's design assumes a session is always seeded from an existing, already-`Ready` `BettingSlip` — chosen because it needs zero new candidate-entry UI. Flagged explicitly as a recommendation, not a decided requirement.

**Current State:** No Planner entry UI exists yet. E-03's slip builder is the only existing candidate-entry surface in the product today.

**Available Options:**
1. **Existing-slip-only** (Architecture's assumption): build a `Ready` slip via E-03 first, then choose "Plan this accumulator" as an alternative to "Analyze."
2. **From-scratch planner entry**: a new, separate candidate-entry flow inside the Planner, independent of E-03.
3. **Both**, offered as alternative paths.

**Advantages / Trade-offs:**

| Option | Advantages | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Existing-slip-only | Zero new entry UI; reuses E-03's existing validation/ownership rules entirely; smallest possible implementation | Customer must build a full slip before planning, even if intending to change most of it; frames Planning as secondary, not primary |
| 2. From-scratch | Could support a "planning-first" product framing, if desired | Genuinely new validation/ownership surface duplicating E-03's; a second, slightly-different rule set is the exact kind of drift `ADR-006`/E-03B's centralization was written to prevent |
| 3. Both | Serves both customer intents | Most design/implementation surface of the three; risk of the two paths drifting apart without deliberate discipline |

**Product Implications:** *Customer Experience* — determines the very first screen of the entire journey (the success criteria's own "from the user's first click"). *Implementation* — Option 1 needs no new entry-point code beyond a button/route; Options 2–3 need new validation/ownership work mirroring E-03B's. *Explainability* — unaffected; evaluation/ranking behaves identically regardless. *Future Roadmap* — Option 1 keeps the door open to add Option 2 later without having built it prematurely.

**Recommendation (optional):** Option 1 — the only option requiring no new entry-point implementation, consistent with this repository's stated preference for the smallest boundary that solves the actual problem, and does not foreclose adding a from-scratch flow later.

### DR-04 — Abandoned/Stale Planner Session Retention

**Background:** `U-07.1` OD-5. Sessions the customer abandons or never returns to will accumulate indefinitely without a retention policy.

**Current State:** No retention or automatic-expiry policy exists for any table in this product today. `BettingSlip`'s own `Archived` status is customer-visible and reversible-by-choice, not automatic deletion.

**Available Options:**
1. **Retain indefinitely** — matches `BettingSlip`'s current no-deletion-only-archival philosophy.
2. **Auto-abandon on inactivity** — mark sessions `Abandoned` after N days with no edit; still retained, not deleted.
3. **Periodic purge** of old `Abandoned` sessions after a retention window.

**Advantages / Trade-offs:**

| Option | Advantages | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Retain indefinitely | Simplest; no scheduled process; consistent with `BettingSlip` | Unbounded storage growth; a stale session lingers as "in progress" indefinitely |
| 2. Auto-abandon | Keeps the customer's workspace tidy without deleting anything | Needs a scheduled job and a chosen inactivity window; a returning customer may find their session already closed |
| 3. Periodic purge | Bounds storage growth over time | Genuine data loss, in tension with this product's general no-silent-discard stance (`SlipAnalysis` immutability, archive-not-delete); needs its own retention-window decision and likely a customer notice |

**Product Implications:** *Customer Experience* — mainly workspace tidiness. *Implementation* — Options 2–3 need a scheduled command, trivial relative to the rest of the programme. *Explainability* — not directly relevant. *Future Roadmap* — an operational decision more than a customer-facing one.

**Recommendation (optional):** Defer until closer to Engineering implementation — genuinely non-blocking for UX Studio's design work, and reversible later.

**Note added 2026-07-26 (`PD-008`):** `PD-008`'s stated principle that "previous revisions remain available for comparison," in service of "complete auditability," is in direct tension with Option 3 (periodic purge) for whichever data actually constitutes those revisions — a purge policy would need an explicit carve-out for revision history, or Option 3 would contradict a decision Product Office has already made. This does not resolve DR-04 (it is about *abandoned* sessions specifically, not revision retention within an active or completed one), but it should inform whichever option is eventually chosen.

### DR-05 — Source Slip Editability During an Open Planning Session — **RESOLVED (`PD-008`, 2026-07-26)**

**Resolution:** `PD-008` selects **Option 3 — Hard lock**: "the original slip is read-only" for the duration of an active Planner session, recorded in `docs/00-governance/DECISION_LOG.md`.

**Reconciliation with the existing architecture, stated plainly rather than assumed:** `PD-008` also describes a "working copy" the Planner operates on, distinct from the original slip. This is not new architecture — it is exactly what `U-07.1` §6 already specified ("`PlannerSelection` rows: snapshot of normalized facts... the source slip is never mutated by this step"). `PD-008`'s hard-lock requirement adds one thing genuinely beyond what `U-07.1` decided: the source slip is now also locked against **direct edits via the existing E-03 builder** while a session is open, not merely left unmutated by the Planner's own internal mechanics — this is the actual, specific resolution of DR-05's question.

**One open point flagged, not resolved here, per "no architecture changes" on this decision:** `PD-008` states the customer "may adopt any revision as the preferred outcome," implying access to more than just the latest cycle. `U-07.1`'s `PlannerRegenerationEvent` history already retains every past cycle's full snapshot (§6), so nothing here is unreachable — but whether "adopting an old revision" means the customer manually re-recreates it through further edits (already fully supported today, no new mechanism needed) or a dedicated one-click "restore to this revision" action (a small addition to `ADR-009`'s Required Call Flow, which today only describes forward-moving re-evaluation) is an implementation-convenience question, not a structural one. Recommend Engineering's eventual implementation handover confirm which is intended, rather than assuming either silently.

**Background (original, retained for context):** `U-07.1` OD-6. A session's candidates are snapshotted at creation, not live-synced with the source `BettingSlip`. If the customer can still directly edit that slip through E-03 while a derived session remains open, the two can silently diverge. **Now active, not conditional:** `DR-03`'s resolution to Option 1 (existing-slip-only, `PD-007`) means this decision applies directly — every Planner session is sourced from an existing slip, so this risk is real for every session, not a hypothetical contingent on a future choice.

**Current State:** Nothing prevents editing a `Ready` slip today (`Ready → Draft`, edit, `Draft → Ready` is already legal) — because the Planner does not exist yet, this risk is currently theoretical, not yet observed.

**Available Options:**
1. **No restriction** — the source slip remains freely editable via E-03 at any time.
2. **Soft warning** — editing shows a warning that an in-progress plan exists, but does not block the edit.
3. **Hard lock** — the source slip cannot be edited via E-03 while a derived session is open; the customer must finish or abandon it first.

**Advantages / Trade-offs:**

| Option | Advantages | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| 1. No restriction | No new restriction on an existing, working feature | Real risk of silent divergence and customer confusion |
| 2. Soft warning | Resolves the confusion risk without removing any existing capability | Needs a new cross-feature awareness check in E-03's builder, plus warning copy |
| 3. Hard lock | Eliminates the divergence risk entirely; simplest mental model | Restricts an existing, already-shipped capability; needs the same cross-feature awareness as Option 2, plus a clear way to resolve the lock |

**Product Implications:** *Customer Experience* — shapes how "in-progress planning" feels alongside the existing builder; Option 3 is the most different from today's product. *Implementation* — Options 2–3 both require E-03's builder to become aware of Planner session state — a small new coupling `U-07.1`'s design does not currently include (it keeps the two contexts one-directional). *Explainability* — Option 3 needs a clear, honest reason shown for the lock. *Future Roadmap* — only relevant if DR-03 keeps the existing-slip-sourced model.

**Recommendation (optional):** Resolve DR-03 first. If resolved as recommended there, Option 2 here addresses the actual risk without restricting an already-shipped feature.
