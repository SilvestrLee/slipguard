## 2. Answers to the Five Explicit Questions

### Question 1 — Does ADR-008 remain fully compatible with PD-007 and PD-008?

**Yes, with zero incompatible statements.** `ADR-008`'s entire scope is the purity of the planner's *evaluation* logic (candidate legs → `CalculateStructuralRisk`, read-only, no persistence in that layer) — it says nothing about where candidates come from (`PD-007`'s concern) or about the source slip's editability (`PD-008`'s concern). The two decisions operate on different layers and never touch the same sentence. No update to `ADR-008` was made, because none is required.

### Question 2 — Does ADR-009 remain fully compatible with PD-007 and PD-008?

**Compatible everywhere except one statement, now identified precisely and reconciled (not silently left).** The incompatible statement was `ADR-009`'s original, single-option description of export as unconditionally overwriting the source slip — written before `PD-008` existed, and in tension with `PD-008`'s framing of that same slip as a permanent "baseline reference" that is "evidence," distinct from Planner "analysis." Every other part of `ADR-009` — the `PlannerSessionStatus` lifecycle, the immutable `PlannerRegenerationEvent` history, the "never touches `SlipAnalysis`" resolution — was already fully compatible with `PD-007`/`PD-008` and required no change. `PD-008`'s own "working copy" language, in particular, is not new architecture at all — it is `ADR-009`'s own `PlannerSelection` snapshot design, confirmed rather than altered (this was already established when `PD-008` itself was recorded, `docs/00-governance/DECISION_LOG.md`).

### Question 3 — Can every inconsistency be resolved through clarification alone, or does any require redesign?

**Two of three were resolved through clarification alone; the third requires neither clarification nor redesign — it requires a Product Office choice between two already-fully-specified options.** Distinguishing these precisely, since the question asks for objective justification, not a blanket answer:

- **Resolved by clarification alone (no redesign, no decision needed):** the source-slip lock representation (Topic B) and the `DR-02` regeneration-cycle count (a missing cross-reference). Neither changes what the architecture does — both simply state, explicitly, a fact that was already true of the existing design but had never been written down. Both are now added directly to `ADR-009`.
- **Requires a Product Office choice, not architectural redesign:** the export-target question (Topic A). This is not "redesign" in the sense of inventing new capability or a new mechanism — both options use the existing `SaveBettingSlip` action and the existing `PlannerSession`/`PlannerSelection` model, and Option 2 adds nothing more than one nullable foreign key. It is also not resolvable by wording alone, because the two options produce genuinely different, mutually exclusive data flows (one slip mutated over time vs. two permanently distinct slips) — no phrasing reconciles that difference; only a decision does.

### Question 4 — Does Engineering currently lack sufficient constitutional guidance because of these inconsistencies?

**Only at the one export step, and only until Product Office picks Option 1 or Option 2.** Both options are now fully specified in `ADR-009` (§ below and the ADR itself) — Engineering is not being asked to wait for further discovery, only for a selection between two already-complete alternatives. Nowhere else in the current design would Engineering be forced to invent product behaviour: the lock mechanism and the cycle-count source, the two other gaps `U-07.3` found, are both closed by this reconciliation.

### Question 5 — After reconciliation, will ADR-008 and ADR-009 become constitutionally complete?

**`ADR-008`: yes, unconditionally — it was already complete and required no reconciliation.** **`ADR-009`: yes, conditionally on the single export-target choice** — once Product Office selects Option 1 or Option 2, nothing else stands between `ADR-009` and acceptance. This report does not make that choice; making it would be inventing a Product Decision, which this work package's own constraints and `docs/offices/ARCHITECTURE_OFFICE.md`'s Prohibited Actions both forbid.

## 3. Mandatory Reconciliation Topics

### Topic A — Export Behaviour: wording issue, sequencing issue, or genuine inconsistency?

**Neither a wording issue nor a sequencing issue — a genuine fork between two coherent architectures, now named precisely rather than resolved by assumption.** `ADR-009` has been updated to state both options in full (Option 1: update the source slip in place; Option 2: export creates a new slip, source slip permanently untouched), each with its own concrete consequences, and to state explicitly that this is the one item blocking acceptance. This is "resolved accordingly" in the sense the handover asked for: the ambiguity is eliminated (both paths are now completely specified, so Engineering has no guesswork whichever is chosen) even though the *choice* itself is correctly left to Product Office.

### Topic B — Hard Lock Behaviour: architectural definition only

**Resolved as a computed fact, not a new stored flag — added directly to `ADR-009`.** A `BettingSlip` is locked against direct editing if and only if a non-terminal `PlannerSession` references it as its source. This is the same architectural pattern `BettingSlip::analysisEligibility()` already established for a different question (E-03B) — a value derived fresh from current state each time it's checked, never cached or duplicated into a column that could silently drift out of sync. No UI behaviour, no copy, and no implementation detail is specified here, per the handover's own instruction — only the structural fact and where it lives.

Each of `U-07.3`'s four identified assumptions, individually dispositioned:

| `DR-04` "deferred" silently implying unbounded retention | **Remains an explicit, still-open Product Decision — not removed, not resolved here.** This is `DR-04` itself (`docs/02-architecture/U-07.2-PLANNER-PRODUCT-DECISION-REGISTER.md`); this report does not close it, only reaffirms that a genuine decision, not silence, is still owed. |

**No implicit Planner behaviour remains after this table**, per the handover's own success criterion — every assumption `U-07.3` found now has a named, explicit home (architecture or Product Decision), none were silently dropped.

## 5. Acceptance Recommendation

**Conclusion B, precisely as the handover's own framework defines it — not a maybe, and not withheld speculatively:**

> **Specific unresolved constitutional inconsistency:** `ADR-009`'s export step has two coherent, fully-specified, mutually exclusive designs (Option 1: update-in-place; Option 2: export creates a new slip). **Why reconciliation cannot go further than this:** the choice depends entirely on a product judgement — whether a customer's originally-entered slip should remain permanently inspectable after planning concludes, or whether the planned result simply becomes that same slip's final state — which is not an architectural question and this office is constitutionally prohibited from deciding it unilaterally. **What Product Office decision is required:** select Export Option 1 or Export Option 2 in `ADR-009`.

Once that single selection is made: `ADR-008` and `ADR-009` may both be recorded `Accepted` in `docs/00-governance/DECISION_LOG.md` in the same action, and per this handover's own stated intent, the Planner architecture becomes constitutionally frozen from that point — this report identifies no further architectural work of any kind after that one choice.
