## 0. What this document is, and its evidentiary limits

This is real, sourced research into actual, currently-operating football data providers — not a hypothetical evaluation. Every pricing/coverage figure below was retrieved via live web search and direct page fetches on 2026-07-29 and is cited. Where a fetch was blocked (several providers return `403 Forbidden` to automated fetches — noted explicitly per provider) the underlying finding still comes from search-indexed content of the same official page or a named secondary source, and is labelled accordingly rather than presented with false confidence.

**This document does not include a live API trial** (§24 of the commission asks for one). Signing up for a provider trial requires creating an account under a real identity/payment method, which this assessment does not have standing authorization to do on the founder's behalf. **This is named as the single most important next step, not skipped silently** — see §9.

No provider is contracted, no credential is generated or stored, and no code was written.

## 1. Executive Recommendation

## **NARROW**

Real, commercially-viable providers exist that can plausibly support a bounded football-only MVP. But two real, unresolved risks — an explicit betting-platform licensing caveat from the most-featured provider, and unconfirmed European-market coverage depth from the odds-specialist candidate — mean Capability B should proceed only with a narrowed scope (fewer leagues, no odds-dependent construction at first) and only after the specific legal questions in §7 are answered. This is not a GO in the sense of "any candidate is immediately safe to contract," and it is not a NO-GO — real, affordable, technically-coherent options exist.

## 5. Coverage assessment

All four candidates cover the "top five" European leagues (EPL, La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A, Ligue 1) at some tier — football-data.org's free tier already includes EPL, La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A, Ligue 1, Eredivisie, Primeira Liga, Championship, Brazilian Série A, World Cup and Euros. No candidate's public marketing specifically confirmed Nigerian domestic league coverage (NPFL) in this pass — flagged as **unknown, not assumed absent**; this should be a direct trial question (§9) if Nigerian-competition coverage is a product requirement, since none of the fetched pages named it either way.

## 6. Taxonomy compatibility — the real open question

This is the single most consequential unresolved technical finding. `FootballMarketTaxonomyV1` requires exact-alias matching against nine market families (Match Result, Double Chance, Draw No Bet, Total Goals, Both Teams to Score, Team Total Goals, Correct Score, Half-Time Result, Half-Time/Full-Time). **No provider's public marketing or pricing page in this pass itemised which of these nine families it actually returns as structured, machine-readable markets** — general phrases like "460+ markets" or "odds from 50+ bookmakers" are marketing claims, not a field-level schema. This cannot be resolved by more searching; it requires inspecting a real sample payload (§9's recommended next step), exactly as the commission's own §14 anticipated. **No taxonomy-compatibility claim in this document should be read as confirmed** — the matrix above states "confirmed via marketing pages" only where a specific market name was named in provider copy, and even that is one step short of a real payload.

## 7. Legal and licence findings — the compliance flag that matters most

**API-Football/API-Sports' own terms, per search-indexed content, state that use of their data for betting platforms may require additional licences from the relevant rights holders, and that no commercial rights on competitions are granted by default.** This is the single most important compliance finding in this document. SlipGuard is not a bookmaker and never places bets (`ADR-012`), but a tool that ingests fixture/market data specifically to *construct an accumulator candidate* sits closer to "betting platform" in spirit than a simple fixture-schedule display would — this needs an explicit legal read on whether SlipGuard's specific use (deterministic candidate construction, customer reviews and accepts, no execution) requires the additional licence API-Football's terms reference, before any contract is signed with this or any similar provider. This exact question should be Compliance Office's first task upon any GO/NARROW decision, per `SD-001`'s own already-recorded recommendation for external legal review.

No equivalent explicit betting-platform clause was found in football-data.org's, SportMonks', or The Odds API's public pages in this pass — **this is recorded as "not found," not as "confirmed absent."** Their full terms of service were not exhaustively fetched (two returned `403` to automated access; two were not attempted this pass) and must be read in full by Compliance/legal before any contract, regardless of what this document did or didn't find.

## 8. MVP recommendation

Football only, top 5 European leagues only, one primary provider. Between the two most technically-promising candidates:

- **The Odds API** is the stronger candidate specifically for *odds/market* data — pricing is transparent, historical odds are included at the entry paid tier ($30/mo), and its own soccer-specific marketing names 1X2/Over-Under/BTTS for EU-region bookmakers, which is the closest match to the required taxonomy families found in this pass. Its general documentation caveat that "spreads and totals are mainly available for US sports" creates real ambiguity about depth of EU soccer market coverage that only a live trial can resolve.
- **SportMonks** is the stronger candidate for *fixture/competition/team* metadata breadth (2,500+ leagues claimed, structured entity model implied by its "2,000+ calls per entity per hour" pricing language), with odds available only as a separate discounted add-on bundle whose exact market list was not itemised on its public pricing page.

**Recommendation: trial both** (§9) before committing to either as the sole MVP provider, since they are not necessarily mutually exclusive — fixture/competition metadata from one, odds/market structure from the other, is a legitimate two-provider MVP shape if a single provider doesn't cover both well enough on inspection.

## 9. Required next step before any GO decision hardens

A bounded, free-tier trial validation exercise (the commission's own §24) — sign up for the free/trial tier of SportMonks and The Odds API, pull a small real sample (2–3 fixtures, priority markets), and inspect the actual field-level payload against `FootballMarketTaxonomyV1`'s nine market families. **This has not been performed as part of this document** — it requires creating real accounts, which needs the founder's explicit go-ahead (even a free tier typically requires an email and, per SportMonks' own trial terms found in search, a credit card for the 14-day trial). Recommend the founder either authorize this specific step explicitly, or perform the signup personally and share a redacted sample payload back to Parser Office for the taxonomy-mapping assessment §6 above could not complete from public marketing pages alone.

## 10. Architecture feedback to U-17.1

`U-17.1`'s recommendation to build the smallest viable operational evidence store (not a "warehouse") and to defer fuzzy identity matching until a real second provider exists is reinforced, not weakened, by this research — real per-provider field naming differences are visible even at the marketing-copy level (e.g. "h2h" vs "1X2" for the same market), confirming identity/taxonomy mapping will be real work, not a formality. `U-17.1`'s recommendation that odds-context dependency be deferred from MVP eligibility/ranking (Decision Register #7) is now more clearly the right call given §6's finding that no provider's odds-market taxonomy fit is actually confirmed yet — building ranking logic against unverified market shapes would risk the exact "solving a business problem with unproven complexity" pattern this codebase has already flagged against elsewhere.

Return to **Product Office** with the NARROW recommendation above. If Product Office wishes to proceed: (1) authorize the §9 trial signup explicitly, naming who performs it; (2) commission Compliance Office to review the §7 betting-platform licensing question and the full ToS of whichever provider(s) survive the trial, before any contract; (3) only then commission Engineering for `U-17.1`'s Phase 1 (canonical identity/provider adapter shape).
