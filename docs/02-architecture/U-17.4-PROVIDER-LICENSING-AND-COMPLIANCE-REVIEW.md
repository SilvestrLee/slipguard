## 2. Provider decision: The Odds API

## **Provider Cleared, with conditions**

Real terms text (`the-odds-api.com/terms-and-conditions.html`, mirrored at a linked, publicly-published Google Doc — the primary domain itself twice returned a connection timeout to direct fetch in this pass, so this assessment relies on the mirrored document plus search-indexed summaries of the same source, disclosed as a lower-confidence path exactly as `U-17.2` disclosed its own blocked fetches) shows:

- **No betting-platform, gambling-service, or advisory-service restriction of any kind** — a materially different posture from API-Football/API-Sports' own terms (`U-17.2` §7), which explicitly require an additional rights-holder licence for betting-platform use. This is the single most consequential finding of this review: **the provider whose terms would have been the hardest blocker is not the one `U-17.3`'s real trial data recommended anyway.**
- **The one real, binding restriction**: do not resell or redistribute the data *as a standalone data product* (a data dump, a re-exposed API, a downloadable feed). Building a user-facing application — dashboard, analytical tool, or (per the provider's own encouraging language found in search) a commercial product — is explicitly the intended and supported use case, provided the data itself isn't the thing being sold. SlipGuard's actual product (a structural-risk analysis and accumulator-construction tool, never a raw-data reseller) sits squarely on the permitted side of this line.
- **Silent, not prohibited, on**: caching duration limits, attribution requirements, and derived-data/derivative-analysis rights. Silence is not permission, but it is also not the explicit prohibition API-Football's terms contained — this is a real gap, not a real blocker, and the honest, defensible path is a direct written clarification request (§7) before production launch, not an assumption in either direction.
- **Liability disclaimer, genuinely favourable to SlipGuard's own architecture**: the provider explicitly disclaims responsibility for "any products created by users" and recommends independent verification of data accuracy — consistent with, not in tension with, SlipGuard's own Locked Decision that its deterministic engine (not any external data source) is solely responsible for every structural finding it produces.
- **Termination risk is standard and operational, not compliance-specific**: access may be revoked "at any time without warning" if abuse is suspected — an availability/architecture-resilience concern (feeds into §8 below), not a licensing blocker.

**Condition attached to this clearance**: before any production integration, send the direct written enquiry in §7 covering caching duration, attribution, and derived-analysis retention — the three real silences above — and receive a written answer, kept as programme evidence exactly as `U-17.2`/`U-17.3` already established the norm of citing dated, real sources rather than assumptions.

## 4. Derived intelligence assessment

SlipGuard's own architecture already draws a clean line the provider's terms happen to reinforce: **the provider supplies raw evidence (fixtures, markets, prices); SlipGuard's own deterministic engine (`CalculateStructuralRisk`, `RankLegsByStructuralWeakness`, unmodified by any of this programme) transforms that evidence into structural scores, risk bands, weakest-leg findings, and plain-language explanations.** Nothing found in the provider's terms treats that transformation as a restricted derivative work — the "no resale as a data product" clause targets re-exposing the *provider's own facts*, not publishing SlipGuard's own calculated findings about them. This assessment (not a legal one) is that derived structural-risk output is SlipGuard's own intellectual product, built on licensed input data, in the same relationship SlipGuard already has with a customer's own manually-entered slip data.

## 5. Betting-platform / regulatory classification — the real open question, explicitly not resolved here

This is the one section of this document where an internal assessment is deliberately *not* offered as sufficient, because the honest answer is that it turns on jurisdiction-specific law this document has no authority to interpret for the founder's actual business.

**What was found, for orientation only, not conclusion**:

- **Nigeria**: a real, significant, and current regulatory disruption was found — a Nigerian Supreme Court decision (reported 22 November 2024) held that lotteries, betting, and gaming are constitutionally reserved to *state* governments, not the federal government, materially undercutting the National Lottery Regulatory Commission's federal licensing framework outside the Federal Capital Territory. This means the regulatory landscape for any gambling-adjacent Nigerian product is genuinely unsettled at a constitutional level right now, not merely under-researched — a materially higher-stakes situation than a stable, well-documented licensing regime would be, and exactly the kind of live legal question this document cannot and should not resolve on its own.
- **United Kingdom**: the Gambling Commission's own guidance (per search-indexed summaries of its "Betting: advice for remote, non-remote and betting intermediaries" page) indicates that a tipster service *placing bets on a customer's behalf* falls within the Gambling Act's licensing trigger. **SlipGuard's own Locked Decisions (`ADR-012`) already forbid exactly that** — no autonomous betting, no bookmaker-account access, no fund custody, every wager requires a conscious customer action outside SlipGuard entirely. This is a genuinely favourable distinguishing fact, grounded in real regulatory text, not a fabricated reassurance — but it is one data point in a classification question that has many others (candidate *construction*, not just *placement*, is new territory `U-17.1` itself named as materially different from Capability A).

**What this document recommends, not concludes**: neither finding above is sufficient, on its own or together, to declare Capability B clear to launch in either jurisdiction. Both should go into the external counsel brief (§12) essentially verbatim, as the starting orientation for someone qualified to give an actual opinion — not as this document's own conclusion.

## 6. Approved language register

Directly extending `DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md`'s existing Brand Tone rules and `ADR-012`'s existing boundaries — no new principle invented, only Capability B-specific vocabulary added to an existing, already-approved system.

**Preferred**: accumulator candidate, planning brief, supported upcoming fixtures, structurally suitable candidate, customer-defined constraints, deterministic eligibility, evidence availability, structural-risk ceiling, candidate review, customer decision, indicative odds, time-stamped odds, no valid candidate.

**Requires caution, case-by-case**: "favourable," "recommended," "strong pick" — none of these are automatically prohibited by `DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md`'s existing rules, but each risks reading as outcome-oriented rather than structure-oriented; prefer the structural-risk vocabulary above wherever a caution-list word is being reached for.

**Prohibited, matching existing Locked Decisions exactly, not a new rule**: guaranteed, sure bet, banker, fixed match, risk-free, cannot lose, winning ticket, safe odds, guaranteed accumulator, safe bet — every one of these is already covered by `CLAUDE.md`'s existing "No 'safe bet' or guaranteed-win claims" Locked Decision; this section names Capability B-specific phrasings of the same already-forbidden idea, not a new restriction.

**Approved candidate-ready wording**: "Candidate ready for your review." **Explicitly rejected**: "Your accumulator is ready to bet" — implies an action SlipGuard never takes and never causes.

**Approved no-result wording**: "No accumulator candidate satisfies all of your current planning constraints." Followed by a specific, honest reason (target odds too high for the selected competitions/window, insufficient supported fixtures, required markets unavailable) — never a suggestion to weaken constraints ("You may revise the planning brief if you choose" is fine; "Increase your risk level to continue" is not, matching `ADR-012`'s "no pressure mechanics, urgency manipulation" boundary exactly).

## 7. Recommended written enquiry to The Odds API (not yet sent)

> "We build a deterministic sports-decision-intelligence product. We do not place bets, access bookmaker accounts, hold customer funds, or guarantee outcomes. We intend to cache fixture/market/odds data briefly to support customer-reviewed accumulator-candidate construction, and to retain our own derived structural-risk findings independent of the underlying raw data. Could you confirm: (1) any maximum caching duration for API responses; (2) whether attribution is required in a customer-facing product; (3) whether our own derived structural-risk scores and explanations, calculated from your data but not reproducing it, may be retained and displayed after the underlying API response would otherwise expire?"

Recommend this be sent by the founder (or an authorized representative) before production integration begins — not fabricated or assumed answered here.

## 8. Caching and freshness — architecture constraints

Given the terms' silence rather than prohibition (§2), the conservative, defensible position pending a written answer: cache raw provider responses briefly (hours, not days) for operational efficiency only; retain SlipGuard's own *derived* structural-risk findings (scores, bands, explanations) indefinitely as SlipGuard's own product data, clearly separated from the raw provider payload's own retention — this also happens to be the shape `U-17.1`'s own evidence-store recommendation (§5 there) already anticipated on independent architectural grounds, so no redesign is required, only this compliance framing layered on top of it.

## 9. Public demonstration boundary

Recommend the safer path `U-17.1`/`PO-U17.0-001`'s own demo-workspace precedent already established: **fictional demo fixtures for any public-facing homepage, screenshot, or marketing content** — never live provider odds, never real bookmaker branding — exactly matching how the existing Demo Workspace (`PO-U17.0-001`) already uses fictional-but-realistic slip data rather than any real external source. This sidesteps the caching/attribution silence in §2 entirely for anything public-facing, and is a stricter, safer position than the provider's terms would technically require — deliberately, since public exposure carries more classification risk (§5) than internal development does.

## 10. Responsible-use controls recommended (extending, not replacing, existing ones)

`ADR-012`'s existing boundaries (no urgency mechanics, no loss-chasing prompts, no pressure to weaken constraints) already cover most of what this section would otherwise invent. Two genuinely new, Capability-B-specific recommendations: (1) a visible, honest odds-may-have-changed disclosure wherever a constructed candidate's odds are shown, since real trial data (`U-17.3`) confirmed prices are bookmaker-supplied and time-stamped, not live-guaranteed; (2) a leg-count ceiling for generated candidates matching the existing Builder's own 20-leg limit — no new ceiling invented, the existing one reused.

## 11. Architecture / Engineering / UX / Data Science boundaries

- **Architecture**: raw provider responses cached briefly (hours); derived structural findings retained separately and indefinitely as SlipGuard's own data; no bookmaker trademark/logo storage or display; revalidate odds before showing a constructed candidate if meaningful time has passed since construction.
- **Engineering**: implement the §7 clarification's eventual answer once received — do not assume an answer and build around a guess; do not build any bookmaker deep-link or affiliate mechanism (explicitly out of scope, matching `ADR-012` and this commission's own §30/§43).
- **UX Studio**: use the §6 approved language register verbatim rather than inventing new copy; the no-result state and odds-disclosure wording above are ready to use as-is.
- **Data Science Lab**: candidate ranking must never use personalised behavioural history (prior losses, journal patterns) to influence which candidate is constructed unless separately, explicitly authorised — matching `PD-007`'s existing "customer supplies everything, SlipGuard never infers intent" principle for Capability A, extended here to Capability B's own construction step.

## 12. External counsel brief (prepared, not sent)

A bounded brief is ready to hand to qualified external counsel, covering: what SlipGuard is and isn't (per `CLAUDE.md`'s own Product Identity and Locked Decisions, unedited); the Capability A/B distinction; the customer-control model (every candidate is reviewed and explicitly accepted, never auto-entered); the specific Nigerian constitutional disruption found in §5; the UK tipster-licensing trigger and why SlipGuard's own architecture doesn't meet it as currently designed; the intended pricing/monetisation model (not yet decided, flagged as open); and The Odds API's own terms as reviewed here. **This document recommends the founder engage counsel with this brief before any public launch of Capability B in any jurisdiction — not before Architecture/Engineering begin internal, non-public build-and-test work,** which this decision (§1) already clears.
