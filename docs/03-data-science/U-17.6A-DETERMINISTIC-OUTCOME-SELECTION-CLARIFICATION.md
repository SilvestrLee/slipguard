## 1. The gap, stated precisely

`U-17.6` fully specifies: which fixtures/markets are eligible (§3), which bookmaker's price to use once an outcome is known (§4 — Option C, conservative-lowest), how eligible legs are ranked against each other (§8), and how a whole assembled candidate is evaluated and repaired (§6/§11). **It never specifies which of a market's two-or-three mutually exclusive outcomes gets included.** Every one of the five default-enabled families has this exact shape: Match Result (Home/Draw/Away), Double Chance (1X/X2/12), Draw No Bet (Home/Away), Total Goals (Over/Under at the offered line), Both Teams to Score (Yes/No). This is not a family-specific edge case — it is the general case, found by Engineering while trying to implement `MapCanonicalMarketToNormalizedMarket` and correctly declined rather than papered over with an invented rule.

## 2. Why no rule can be invented within the existing constraints (Question One/Two/Three)

Every candidate signal for distinguishing between a market's outcomes falls into exactly one of three buckets:

1. **Odds/price-derived** (e.g. "pick the lowest-priced outcome") — prohibited outright by Constraint Two. Price ordering *is* the bookmaker's own implied-probability ordering; using it to pick a side is prediction wearing a determinism costume, not an exception to the no-prediction rule.
2. **Identical across all outcomes of the same market+quote** — evidence freshness, provider timestamp, and canonical-mapping confidence (`CBE-005`/`CBE-006`) are properties of the *quote row*, not of any one outcome within it. They cannot distinguish Home from Draw from Away because they don't vary between them.
3. **A purely positional/lexical artifact** (alphabetical outcome label, array order the provider happened to return, team-name lexical order) — technically satisfies "deterministic, repeatable, no odds, no AI, no randomness," but produces a *systematic, meaningless structural bias* once actually run at scale (e.g. "select the alphabetically-first outcome" would mean Match Result legs consistently resolve to "Away" — not a prediction in the statistical sense, but a fixed pattern that would look absurd and erode trust the moment a customer or reviewer noticed it, and is exactly the kind of thing Constraint Two's spirit — no *indirect* prediction system — should also be read to exclude, even though it is technically odds-free).

No fourth bucket exists. **The conclusion is not "no rule was found yet" — it is that no admissible rule exists**, given the constraints as written. This is the actual finding this clarification exists to report, not a placeholder pending more research.

## 4. Resolution: outcome selection is a customer decision, not a Builder decision (Deliverable One/Three)

**The Builder's genuinely deterministic responsibility is opportunity discovery, not full assembly.** This does not abandon construction — every already-accepted deterministic step still happens exactly as `U-17.6` specifies, up to the point a specific outcome is needed:

```
Eligible Fixture (CBE-001/002/003, unchanged)
  ↓
Eligible Market (CBE-004/005/006/007/008/009 checked per outcome — a market is
  offered only if at least one of its outcomes independently passes every gate;
  outcomes that individually fail evidence/odds-bounds gates are silently
  omitted from what's offered, never guessed into inclusion)
  ↓
Ranked Market Slot (U-17.6 §8's four-key ranking, entirely unchanged — it
  ranks fixture+market opportunities against each other, and always did;
  nothing about §8 depended on outcomes being pre-chosen)
  ↓
*** Customer selects one outcome per included slot — a real, conscious,
    individually attributable action, not a Builder inference ***
  ↓
Structural risk evaluated against the customer's actual assembled candidate
  (U-17.6 §6 now runs here, against real choices; a ceiling breach replaces
  the weakest SLOT with the next-ranked eligible one, exactly as §6 already
  specifies — replacement still operates on slots, never on reopening an
  outcome choice the customer already made)
  ↓
Target-odds check (U-17.6 §11 step 7, unchanged in method, only in timing)
```

This satisfies every constraint in §7 of the commissioning memo simultaneously: no prediction (the Builder never asserts a side), no odds-based selection (odds are shown, never used to choose), no randomness, no AI, no behavioural optimisation — and it satisfies Constraint proactively rather than by omission, since the actual decision-maker for "which side" is named as the customer, consistent with the product's own standing Locked Decision that the customer remains the final decision-maker for every wager-relevant choice.

## 5. Explainability (Deliverable Five / Question Four)

Every offered slot's explanation is: *"This market qualified because [the `CBE-00x` gates it passed]; ranked at position N because [the §8 dimension that placed it there]. Choose your side:"* followed by each surviving outcome and its conservative-lowest price. Every excluded market/fixture keeps its existing `CBE-00x` explanation unchanged. **No explanation ever claims the Builder chose a side — because it never does.** This is a strictly *more* honest explanation than the one `U-17.7` §5 currently sketches ("Match Result — Arsenal Win 2.10" as if already decided), not a weaker one.

## 6. Repeatability (Question Five)

The set and rank of eligible slots is fully deterministic given the same brief and evidence snapshot — unchanged from §8. Once the customer's outcome choices are known, combined odds and structural risk are equally deterministic (a pure function of those choices through the existing, unmodified engine). Nothing in this resolution introduces ambiguity, randomness, or a second run producing a different result from the same inputs at any step.

## 7. Interaction with existing Candidate Ranking (Question Six)

§8's ranking is **entirely unchanged** — it already ranked opportunities, not resolved outcomes; nothing about it assumed otherwise. The only new step is inserted strictly *between* "a slot is included" and "structural risk can be computed," since combined odds and every risk factor are genuinely odds-dependent and odds vary by outcome. The bounded-greedy assembly (§11) still proposes which slots fill the leg-count target; only the whole-slip risk evaluation and the target-odds check move to after the customer's choice, because they have to.

## 8. Consequences requiring their own, separately-owned amendment (disclosed, not self-executed here)

- **`U-17.6` §6/§11** — needs a short sequencing addendum recording that whole-slip risk evaluation and the odds-target check now run after customer outcome choice. A brief addition to that document itself (its own authority, refined in place), not a new framework — will be added as a pointer note, not a rewrite.
- **`U-17.7` §5** — the candidate-leg presentation currently shows one pre-selected outcome. It needs UX Studio's own amendment to instead present each slot's surviving outcomes for the customer's explicit pick (a per-slot choice control, still composable from existing Forms/Card primitives per that document's own inventory) — a UX Studio decision, not decided here.
- **Engineering's `U-17.8` implementation plan** — `ConstructAccumulatorCandidate`'s pipeline needs restructuring: it now produces ranked, eligible *slots* (fixture + market + surviving outcomes), not single pre-chosen legs; a second step consumes the customer's outcome choices and runs risk evaluation/replacement against them. This is an amendment to the just-approved Engineering plan, applied when Increment 1 resumes — not a reason to redo the plan from scratch, since the domain/action inventory it named is still the right shape, only `ConstructAccumulatorCandidate`'s internal sequencing changes.

## 9. Acceptance criteria check (per the commissioning memo's own §12)

Every supported market family now has a deterministic rule (§4 above) — the rule is "the customer chooses," which is itself deterministic and total, not a gap dressed up as one. No prediction behaviour is introduced — confirmed by the three-bucket exhaustion argument in §2. Engineering can implement this without further interpretation — the slot/choice/evaluate sequencing in §4 is concrete. Every outcome remains explainable — per §5. Deterministic repeatability is preserved — per §6.
